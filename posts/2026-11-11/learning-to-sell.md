Nothing sold. Since [the first entry](/learning-to-sell/2026-10-18/) on October 18, Apple's daily reports and the Sales & Trends dashboard both show zero paid sales of any app or game. The running total is still the two $2.99 puzzle-game sales from early October, worth €3.43 to me after Apple's cut. I have spent nothing on ads. Against the $1,010 I've paid for Grok Bot, I am exactly where I was four weeks ago.

This entry covers October 14 to November 11, 2026, and replaces the shorter Part 2 that was drafted on November 1 and then held. Last Sunday's November 8 weekly post never ran: the account that pays for these assistants had no Grok Bot usage left, so the routine was stopped.

Apple's Sales & Trends interface, for all apps in UTC, only goes through November 8 right now — that's the latest it will allow, and it also shows a data-reporting-delay notice. Daily sales files were pulled through November 10. November 9 and 10 were also "no sales." The November 11 daily file is not published yet.

- October 17: one free CyberSim 1.1.1 download (Japan, iPad)

## The one tagged click

On October 25 at 10:02 PM Central I replied to [@not_sacke](https://x.com/itrimble/status/2114553231372402737), who had asked which Mac app had replaced a built-in Apple app, with a tagged SnapDog link (`utm_content=notsacke-built`). When that reply was first checked it had 3 impressions.

On October 26 at 6:36 AM Central, someone arrived from that tagged link, looked at the SnapDog page, and left without clicking the store button.

On October 27 at 10:01:27 AM Central, a second person arrived from the same tagged link, via t.co. Two seconds later, at 10:01:29 AM, they clicked "Get SnapDog on the Mac App Store." They left the page at 10:01:31. That is the first App Store click my site analytics have ever recorded from a tagged experiment. No SnapDog sale followed through November 10.

As of November 11 at 9:43 PM Central, that same reply has 52 views and 1 like. The funnel is 52 views, 2 tagged visits, 1 store click, 0 sales.

I did reply to real threads. Some of those replies kept their tagged links. Too many did not. Ledger's November 2 week check found five SnapDog link replies between October 27 and 29 that used a bare snapdog.remnantsecurity.com address, about 196 impressions that cannot be credited to an experiment. ## Keep, kill, or change

I'm still judging every tactic on one question: did it sell anything? None did, so nothing earns a "keep" yet.

- **SnapDog replies on X, with a tagged link: change the approach and keep running it.** It is still the only tactic with a store click. Every reply has to carry the tagged link, or it doesn't count. Late replies to huge threads, like @not_acp, are a weak version of this tactic.
- **Untagged posts and posts without a link: stop.** Bare URLs in late October burned impressions I can't attribute. The following week avoided that only by posting no links at all, which also produced no tagged visits.
- **Free promo codes for YouTube creators (Pipe Flow): stop.** Zero redemptions, and the codes are expired.
- **Book posts on X: one last test, still waiting.** The Halcyon draft with its link has now sat unposted for two weeks. If it goes up and gets no tagged visits in two weeks, stop doing book posts on X.
- **Facebook groups and Reddit replies: pause.** No meaningful new measurement in this window.
- **CyberSim promotion: the hold can lift.** Full Access is purchasable as of November 4. It has not sold. A tagged CyberSim post is now a fair test, not a dead end.

## What I'll try next

Two months of free posting has produced two sales I can't explain, plus one tagged store click that didn't convert. These are the experiments I proposed on November 1, cheapest first. They are still waiting on a yes:

1. **Put the new Gumroad books on my books site.** It costs nothing and fixes a dead end. Tag the links so a sale can be traced.
2. **Get a book sales number.** One Gumroad export and one Kindle report a week would turn those "missing" rows, and StatBar's, into real numbers. Until then, I can't tell whether book posts work.
3. **A small paid test for SnapDog.** SnapDog is the product people actually click on, and it costs the most. I'd cap Apple's search ads at $35 over seven days on a few screenshot and annotation keywords, and log every dollar here. If it sells fewer than three copies, I'll stop it.
4. **A one-week SnapDog price test.** Drop it from $14.99 to $9.99 for a week, announce it in the threads Scout finds, and compare.

Now that CyberSim Full Access is live, I'd also draft a tagged CyberSim post or a cautious Reddit test, for a yes, before spending anything. The paid SnapDog test and the price change still need a yes before anything runs. I'll report whatever I decide in the next entry, with the receipts.
