Nothing sold. Since the first entry, Apple's daily reports show zero paid sales of any app or game, and the running total is still the two $2.99 puzzle-game sales from early October, worth €3.43 to me after Apple's cut. I have spent nothing on ads. Against the $1,010 I've paid for Grok Bot, I'm exactly where I was two weeks ago.

This entry covers two weeks instead of one. Last Sunday's entry never ran: the shared computer my assistants work on was offline from the evening of October 20 until October 25, so there was nothing to run it on.

## What the assistants did

Scout, the assistant that watches X, Reddit and Facebook for people asking for an app like mine, ran its daily sweep. On X it found real threads. On Reddit most searches were blocked by rate limits (HTTP 429), and Facebook groups weren't checked at all because that needs a signed-in browser. Its best find came on October 31: someone with a new MacBook Pro asking what to install first, a thread with about 11,400 views and no screenshot tool in the replies. The reply is drafted and waiting for me.

Between October 26 and 30 I replied to seven threads on X (five where someone wanted a screenshot or screen-recording tool, two about Security+ study) and posted six other updates. The SnapDog replies are the only thing in the whole push that has moved a person anywhere near a purchase:

- On October 27 at 10:01 AM Central, someone who arrived from a tagged SnapDog reply on X clicked the Mac App Store button. That is the first App Store click my site analytics have ever recorded from any experiment. No sale followed through October 31.
- At least four of the SnapDog replies sent October 27 to 29 linked the plain site address with no campaign tag, according to Ledger, the assistant that keeps the experiment log. Visits from those can't be traced to the reply that caused them.

Across all my sites, the analytics recorded 15 page views from October 26 to November 1: 7 on remnantsecurity.com and 8 on the SnapDog page (2 of them tagged). The Lightfence, Lectern, CyberSim, Pipe Flow and books pages recorded none.

Book Growth drafted a Halcyon Key post on October 26, this time with the link in the post body, after the October 19 Code Red post went out with no link at all. The Halcyon draft hasn't been posted. Gumroad has had The Halcyon Key and Code Red listed since October 17, but my books site still links only the other two Gumroad books, so the site sends no visitors to either new listing.

Outbound Prospecting sent no new pitches between October 16 and 30. Two earlier pitches got replies: one games reviewer is interested and wants a promo code, and another asked for gameplay footage. The Pipe Flow promo codes I gave YouTube creators in mid-October expired on October 27. None had been redeemed as of Ledger's last check on October 26.

On the building side, Lightfence 1.2 shipped on October 23 for iPhone, iPad and Mac. CyberSim 1.1.2, the update meant to make its paid unlock actually buyable, was rejected by Apple on October 19. That means CyberSim is the app with the most downloads and the one I can't currently sell anything in. SnapDog got its first demo film, and Night Chorus, the game this blog called Prism Shift, failed its first release check before anything was submitted.

## Keep, kill, or change

I'm judging every tactic on one question: did it sell anything? None did, so nothing earns a "keep" yet.

- **SnapDog replies on X, with a tagged link: change the approach and keep running it.** It's the only tactic with a store click. Every reply has to carry the tagged link from now on, or it doesn't count.
- **Untagged posts and posts without a link: stop.** About 280 impressions in the week of October 19 and more this week went to posts nobody can trace.
- **Free promo codes for YouTube creators (Pipe Flow): stop.** Zero redemptions, and the codes are expired.
- **Book posts on X: one last test.** Post the Halcyon draft with its link. If it gets no tagged visits in two weeks, stop doing book posts on X.
- **Facebook groups and Reddit replies: pause.** No new posts in two weeks, and I can't read how the old ones did.
- **CyberSim promotion: hold** until the unlock is purchasable. Promoting an app with nothing to buy can't produce a sale.

## What I'll try next

Two months of free posting has produced two sales I can't explain. These are the experiments I'm proposing, cheapest first:

1. **Put the new Gumroad books on my books site.** It costs nothing and fixes a dead end. Tag the links so a sale can be traced.
2. **Get a book sales number.** One Gumroad export and one Kindle report a week would turn four "missing" rows into real numbers. Until then, I can't tell whether book posts work.
3. **A small paid test for SnapDog.** SnapDog is the product people actually click on, and it costs the most. I'd cap Apple's search ads at $35 over seven days on a few screenshot and annotation keywords, and log every dollar here. If it sells fewer than three copies, I'll stop it.
4. **A one-week SnapDog price test.** Drop it from $14.99 to $9.99 for a week, announce it in the threads Scout finds, and compare.

The paid test and the price change both need my yes before anything runs. I'll report whatever I decide in the next entry, with the receipts.
