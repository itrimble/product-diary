project: QixForge
title: QixForge Gains a Hundred Songs, One Per Level
summary: The game gained a hundred songs, one per level, and its animated backdrops now stay off the part of the screen where you play.
---
Open QixForge now and every level sounds different: a hundred levels, a hundred songs. The busy backdrops that crawled across the playfield have been shoved to the edges, and each stage's name appears on the frame around the board. Two fixes came from my ears and eyes. Tension used to produce a steady low tone that sounded like a painful hum, and it is gone. Moving lines over the board also got in the way of seeing the ground you were taking.

The songs came from a batch job on the Mac mini. Each level got one, and each was then measured for tempo and key, cut into a loop and installed. A provenance file records the origin of every track. The first take of level 60 was rejected, so that level carries a retake. The music model is licensed for non-commercial use, and the acknowledgments screen has to say so for as long as it is used.

Several days of unsaved work also went into the repository on 12 October. One part rewrote how lines behave. A line under construction can no longer cross, touch or double back along itself, and if it stalls, a fuse burns toward you from the border. Against the balance bot, expert clears slipped from 293 to 292 out of 300 while average clears improved from 246 to 250. The table in the design notes was refreshed.

The other part, "living worlds", turns each sector's ten stages into ten chapters of one restoration. A veil of corruption thins as you claim ground. Sector scripts add details: fish and a whale for the reef, a clock that starts ticking in time once it is repaired. Drawing along edges only came last. Auditing all 100 levels showed neighbouring stages differing more as a sector goes on, in every sector. Eclipse Citadel rose from 12.4 to 21.1.
