project: Monitor Switch
title: A Stubborn Monitor Was Really a Cable Problem
summary: A monitor that refused to follow the desk switch to the personal laptop was traced to a cable, and unplugging it fixed it.
---
The cure for my stubborn monitor was pulling out one cable. Plenty of cleverer ideas failed first. One of the three monitors on my desk kept showing the work laptop after I flipped the switch box to the personal one, and the only way across was the monitor's own menu. With the docking station's cable out, the switch alone now does the job. No software is involved.

The culprit is a cheap 24-inch onn panel. Its Auto Source mode hops to a new input only once the old one dies, and the Targus DisplayLink dock never lets that happen. It holds the HDMI connection open and sends a blank picture, so as far as the monitor knows, the work laptop is still talking. The test that settled it was simple. Cable out, one press of the switch moves the monitor. Cable back in on another port, and it sticks again.

Software got a fair try first and lost. I tried asking the monitor to change input over its control channel. All three monitors answered with the same I/O error, which makes me suspect that the switch box and the dock both block that line. Shutting down DisplayLink on the work laptop, first its agent and then the app, blanked the picture and changed nothing else. An early test turned out to have run on the wrong machine, so my notes take it back. Another utility crashes outright with the small prompter display attached.

Untested so far: flipping back to the work laptop and then to the personal one again, and whether sleeping the work laptop lets go of the monitor. When the work laptop needs a screen while the switch points elsewhere, the plan is to plug the dock into another monitor. One leftover tool on the personal laptop waits for my say-so before it goes.
