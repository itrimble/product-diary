project: Prism Shift
title: Prism Shift Gets New Music for All Fifteen Levels
summary: All fifteen levels now play brand-new music made on the home computer, and the game was installed on a real phone.
---
Every level of Prism Shift now has its own piece of music: a music-writing program on the Mac mini produced all fifteen, and I switched them on for my own use. Visually each level got a backdrop, a moving landmark, a lighting tint and its own way of cutting crystals. That build is on the phone. Whether the music works during play is a judgment I have not made yet.

This is a homebrew build, and the license says why. The music model allows non-commercial use only, which suits a homebrew project, as I confirmed on 11 October. So these tracks are not cleared for a paid release, and the model's credit has to stay. There is a technical limit as well. The model delivers a finished mix, where the engine normally blends five separate layers. Each track therefore plays as one loop out of `SkinTracks`, with the older layered recordings kept as the fallback.

Production is further along than listening. Drafts, normalized masters and prepared loops exist for all fifteen, and an audit of those files for format and clipping passed. A beat and key review and a five-minute listen have not happened. Asking the model for a tempo proves nothing about the tempo it delivered, so I trusted none of them.

I also wrote up two game modes. Challenge has no clock, and its goals are measured in earned light. Single Skin loops a level you have reached at its own tempo, with no goals and no speed-up, although a full board still ends the run. I left the matching rule and the six-beat sweep alone deliberately: an eight-beat sweep would force the balance numbers to be measured again. Whether matches should become 2×2 squares is still an open question.
