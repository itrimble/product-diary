SnapDog, my Mac app for screenshots and screen recording, did not change on Thursday. What I added is a single presentation slide that tells its story in one glance: an idea to a shipped Mac App Store app, built entirely by talking to an assistant. If you ever wanted to know how long that took, the slide says about 40 hours of hands-on time.

The slide is a standalone web page, a file in the `presentation/` folder, 136 lines. It is a dark, projector-friendly page with a five-step timeline: concept, build, submit, review, approved. Dates run from signing and submitting version 1.0.0 on 12 July, through one question from Apple's reviewers on 13 July about an entitlement, answered in the thread, to approval on 17 July. Four counters sit under it: 13 languages, 19 upload destinations, one review round, and 40 hours.

Two honest notes. First, those figures come from the slide itself and my own earlier notes. I did not re-check them against the store tonight, and the 19 destinations are the same count my [ad research on Wednesday](/snapdog/2026-10-28/) found the live listing does not fully back. Second, it is the story of version 1.0.0, while the store has since moved to 1.2.1.

The only other commit that day was the Wednesday ad draft going into the repository.
