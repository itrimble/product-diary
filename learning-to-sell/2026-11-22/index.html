    Nothing sold. Since the first entry on October 18, Apple's daily reports and the Sales & Trends dashboard both show zero paid sales of any app or game. The running total is still the two $2.99 puzzle-game sales from early October, worth €3.43 to me after Apple's cut. I have spent nothing on ads. Against the $1,010 I've paid for Grok Bot, I am exactly where I was four weeks ago.

    This entry covers October 14 to November 22, 2026, and replaces the shorter Part 2 that was drafted on November 1 and then held. Last Sunday's November 8 weekly post never ran: the account that pays for these assistants had no Grok Bot usage left, so the routine was stopped.

    The one tagged click

    On October 25 at 10:02 PM Central I replied to @not_sacke, who had asked which Mac app had replaced a built-in Apple app, with a tagged SnapDog link (utm_content=notsacke-built). When that reply was first checked it had 3 impressions.

    On October 26 at 6:36 AM Central, someone arrived from that tagged link, looked at the SnapDog page, and left without clicking the store button.

    On October 27 at 10:01:27 AM Central, a second person arrived from the same tagged link, via t.co. Two seconds later, at 10:01:29 AM, they clicked "Get SnapDog on the Mac App Store." They left the page at 10:01:31. That is the first App Store click my site analytics have ever recorded from a tagged experiment. No SnapDog sale followed through November 10.

    As of November 11 at 9:43 PM Central, that same reply has 52 views and 1 like. The funnel is 52 views, 2 tagged visits, 1 store click, 0 sales.

    The tagged SnapDog reply to @not_sacke that later produced the store click. Captured shortly after posting. The timestamp in the capture is 3:02 AM on October 26 UTC, which is 10:02 PM Central on October 25. It had 3 impressions at that first check.

    The same reply, followed all the way through. Views are the public impression count as of November 11, 9:43 PM Central. Visits and the click are from PostHog. Sales are from Apple's daily reports.

    There were three other App Store clicks in the window, none of them tagged, and none of them a sale:

    • November 4 at 10:42 AM Central: an untagged t.co visitor clicked "Get SnapDog" on the features page.
    • November 7 at 7:37 AM Central: two "Download on the App Store" clicks, eight seconds apart, both from a direct visit to the CyberSim page. CyberSim 1.1.2, with the $9.99 Full Access purchase, had been live for three days. No new download and no in-app purchase followed.

    All four App Store button clicks from October 14 to November 11, from PostHog. None became a paid sale.

    Keep, kill, or change

    I'm still judging every tactic on one question: did it sell anything? None did, so nothing earns a "keep" yet.

    • SnapDog replies on X, with a tagged link: change the approach and keep running it. It is still the only tactic with a store click. Every reply has to carry the tagged link, or it doesn't count. Late replies to huge threads, like @not_acp, are a weak version of this tactic.
    • Untagged posts and posts without a link: stop. Bare URLs in late October burned impressions I can't attribute. The following week avoided that only by posting no links at all, which also produced no tagged visits.
    • Free promo codes for YouTube creators (Pipe Flow): stop. Zero redemptions, and the codes are expired.
    • Book posts on X: one last test, still waiting. The Halcyon draft with its link has now sat unposted for two weeks. If it goes up and gets no tagged visits in two weeks, stop doing book posts on X.
    • Facebook groups and Reddit replies: pause. No meaningful new measurement in this window.
    • CyberSim promotion: the hold can lift. The Full Access purchase is purchasable as of November 4. It has not sold. A tagged CyberSim post is now a fair test, not a dead end.

    4. A one-week SnapDog price test. Drop it from $14.99 to $9.99 for a week, announce it in the threads Scout finds, and compare.

    1. A standalone SnapDog 1.2.2 post with a short motion clip. My replies this week got 1 to 4 impressions. My standalone product and build posts got 8 to 55. If it gets 0 tagged visits, stop SnapDog posts on X.
    2. One CyberSim post in r/CompTIA, tagged. The Security+ track is free, and Apple reports free downloads the next day, so this is the cleanest test I have. Use the existing CYBERSIM-REDDIT-01 tag. Measure tagged visits, free downloads and the $9.99 Full Access purchase for 7 days. If there are 0 downloads, stop CyberSim promotion outside the App Store.
    3. Make Pipe Flow available to the one creator who asked for it. A YouTube creator with a first-gameplay channel wants to play Pipe Flow, and his code won't work in Serbia. Check whether Serbia is in Pipe Flow's App Store availability. If it isn't, add it and send him a fresh code. Measure: does a video go up, and do Pipe Flow sales follow within 14 days.
